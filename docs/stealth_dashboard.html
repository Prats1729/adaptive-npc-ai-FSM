The 3 Novel AI Model Architectures

Novel Model 1: Predictive Interception Potential Field (PI-PF)

Scientific Motivation: Xu & Verbrugge (2025) anchor the Information Field at the historical sighting p_t. Because agile evasive players run at 120% guard speed, guards are perpetually chasing outdated scent trails ("ghosts").
Algorithmic Solution: PI-PF estimates instantaneous player velocity v_p = p_t - p_{t-1} and projects the lookahead intercept tile p_pred = p_t + k * v_p (k=2..3). Guards steer directly towards perpendicular escape bottlenecks, securing the fastest capture time on Miami (29.27s).

Novel Model 2: Multi-Agent Coordinated Q-Learning Guard (MARL-Q)

Scientific Motivation: The base paper hand-tunes field weights (wI=0.9, wC=0.5, wN=0.1). In dead-end maze topologies, artificial potential gradients flatten out, stranding guards in local minima.
Algorithmic Solution: Formulated pursuit as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) solved via Bellman TD updates: Q(s, a) ← Q(s, a) + α [R + γ max Q(s', a') - Q(s, a)] with state discretization and macro-actions (Pursue, Explore, Chokepoint, Flank). On Test Maze, RL-Q achieves 92.0% Capture Rate (+36% over base paper) in just 27.93s.

Novel Model 3: Potential-Field Guided Monte Carlo Tree Search (APF-MCTS)

Scientific Motivation: Continuous potential fields operate myopically on 1-hop neighbors. In intricate architectures, guards enter dead ends before realizing the exit is blocked.
Algorithmic Solution: APF-MCTS performs online multi-agent tree search looking ahead H=4 steps into the future using UCB1 node selection. Leaf evaluations use the Composite Potential Field as a dense heuristic rollout value function, eliminating oscillatory traps and achieving 86.0% Capture Rate across both levels.
