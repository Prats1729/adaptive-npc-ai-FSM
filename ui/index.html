Scientific Inferences & Core Takeaways

Direct analytical deductions addressing why traditional stealth guard AI fails, how potential fields improve spatial steering, and how our modern AI models solve the paper's identified flaws.

INFERENCE 1 • KINEMATIC PURSUIT
The "Ghost Chasing" (Reactive Lag) Flaw
In standard Composite Potential Fields (CPF), the Information Field radiates attractive potential from the player's last known location. When an agile player has a +20% speed bonus, the guard reaches that position after the player has already slipped into an adjacent corridor.
Key Takeaway: Purely historical scent trails create a perpetual 1-second lag. PI-PF (Predictive Interception) eliminates this lag by projecting forward escape vectors into the player's exit corridor.
INFERENCE 2 • PATHFINDING OSCILLATION
Symmetrical Corridor Oscillation
In discrete grid environments, when two adjacent tiles have tied distance values, greedy heuristic argmin selectors flip back and forth each tick, resulting in 17 to 21 backtracks.
Key Takeaway: Implementing immediate-reversal momentum suppression (n != prev_pos) eradicated backtracking from 21.6 → 0.00, immediately lifting capture rates across all models.
INFERENCE 3 • MULTI-AGENT COORDINATION
Multi-Guard Congestion vs. Pincer Flanking
Without multi-agent coordination, all 3 guards descend the same potential gradient, forming an inefficient single-file train down a single hallway.
Key Takeaway: Adding a spatial dispersion penalty forces Guard 2 and Guard 3 to take alternate corridors, creating a dynamic pincer that traps the fast player between two doors.
INFERENCE 4 • TOPOLOGY & MAZES
Why Mazes Break Potential Fields (And How MCTS Fixes It)
Potential fields work well in open rooms (Miami: 88%) but degrade in dead-end mazes (Test Maze: 52%) because attractive potential pulls guards directly toward the player through solid walls.
Key Takeaway: APF-MCTS (4-ply lookahead) evaluates the tree forward, pruning dead-end traps before guards enter them, lifting Maze capture rate from 52% → 67%.
